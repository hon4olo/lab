# Cognitive Review Rubric

Use this rubric for fast scoring. Scores are not final truth; they are a forcing function to expose weak areas.

Score each dimension from 1 to 5.

- 1 = blocker
- 2 = major risk
- 3 = acceptable but weak
- 4 = good
- 5 = excellent

## Perception

Questions:

- Can players see what matters?
- Are interactables visually distinct?
- Are threats, rewards, exits, and goals readable?
- Are icons understandable without explanation?
- Is visual hierarchy clear under motion and pressure?

Common fixes:

- Stronger contrast.
- Larger targets.
- Fewer competing UI elements.
- Motion/outline/glow for next action.
- More distinctive silhouettes.

## Memory

Questions:

- Does the player need to remember rules, controls, or goals that could instead be visible?
- Are tutorial messages too long?
- Are rules repeated in context?
- Are objectives and controls accessible?
- Are choices understandable at the moment of decision?

Common fixes:

- Visible goals.
- Short contextual reminders.
- Progressive disclosure.
- Repetition over time.
- Recognition instead of recall.

## Attention

Questions:

- Is attention guided toward the current task?
- Are important signals timed well?
- Is the game asking the player to learn while multitasking?
- Are popups, particles, UI, and effects competing?
- Is critical information available through more than one modality?

Common fixes:

- Reduce simultaneous prompts.
- Teach in calm moments.
- Use motion/contrast sparingly.
- Pair visual and audio cues.
- Sequence tasks one at a time.

## Motivation

Questions:

- Is the current goal clear?
- Does the reward matter?
- Does the player feel competence growth?
- Is there meaningful agency?
- Does progression support the core loop?

Common fixes:

- Clear short-term goal.
- Meaningful reward.
- Visible progress.
- Small choices.
- Mastery milestones.

## Emotion

Questions:

- Does the game evoke the intended feeling?
- Are failures understandable and fair?
- Are success and impact satisfying?
- Are controls responsive enough?
- Does frustration come from challenge or bad UX?

Common fixes:

- Stronger feedback.
- Clear fail causes.
- Better telegraphing.
- Less randomness in early failure.
- More responsive input.

## Learning

Questions:

- Does the player learn by doing?
- Is the first exposure safe?
- Is one concept taught at a time?
- Is mastery practiced before difficulty rises?
- Is punishment delayed until the rule is understood?

Common fixes:

- Interactive tutorial.
- Safe first action.
- One mechanic per beat.
- Practice before pressure.
- Immediate feedback.
